SUBJECT: four frames of one memory, "the earthy hand": an old village woman in a long plain dress, a headscarf and a shawl, a gardener's hands.
Panel 1: a wide empty snowy field in winter, low bare hills far behind, snow falling. The old woman kneels in the snow in the middle, pressing a single green plant stalk upright into the snow with both hands. Lonely, quiet.
Panel 2: the same spot, close: the old woman sits in the snow beside the stalk, which has grown taller with a few leaves, her mouth open singing to it, one hand held over it. Snow still on the ground.
Panel 3: many years later: a young wood of slender trees stands where the field was. The woman, now very old and bent, cuts one single stalk and holds it out to a tall slender WOODEN STAG standing on two legs (a tree-spirit: body of pale wood, a deer-skull face, branching antlers with small blossoms, a mantle of moss; see the leftmost creature in the attached reference image, but draw it in this woodcut style). The stag reaches for the stalk.
Panel 4: close view of the wooden stag's hand (twig fingers) holding the stalk upright, the stalk still green and leafy; the young wood behind; the old woman is gone.
