# Backdrops

**Never break:** a layer is placed only by `BackdropMath`: the region's horizon on an axis where the art has an edge, world 0 on an axis where it repeats. A region has one set and one horizon; a room's `Backdrop` only overrides the set (caves, interiors, rooms inside rock). A backdrop pixel is drawn by exactly one room: the one whose rect holds it, or, in the void, the room `void_owner` picks among the rooms within `RoomRing.WIDTH`: the highest hold (over its top, beside it, under its floor, past a corner), then the nearest, so every dividing line is straight. A seam is a hard cut: where two rooms draw different pictures (`BackdropMath.same_picture`) the cut touches ground, void, a `[back]` wall or a lake (`backdrop_seams_test`; every exception in its `ALLOWED` with a reason). A fill past the strip reads the sheet's own edge row, never a colour. `art_row`/`art_col` and `pixel_centre`/`keeps`/`void_owner`/`rect_distance` in `BackdropMath` and `backdrop_layer.gdshader` are one mapping in two languages: change both. Every room has a backdrop whose first layer is opaque; no room shows the pack forest.

Design 04 section 6 ("Fundos verticais"), WORLD-06, which also fixes WORLD-04. Plan and options: `architecture/vertical-backdrops`.

- **Draw order.** `Backdrop.Z` is -30: behind WORLD-07's `[back]` walls (-20) and props (-10) (`systems/props`).
- **Over its top, the region's sky** (2026-10-13, `bugs/a-wall-climb-over-the-seam-pillar-shows-the-wall-up-cut-in-the-sky`). A `Backdrop` that overrides its region's set builds the override with `Backdrop.Part.UNDER_TOP` and the region's set with `OVER_TOP` (`Sky0..`); the shader's `part` discards by `BackdropMath.over_top`, the pixel centre above the room's top edge (one test in both languages). Rock fills only the room and the void beside or under it; over its wall tops the sky goes on, so two rooms of one region meet in the same pixels there.
- **Authoring** (WORLD-28, 2026-10-13). A region scene sets `Region.backdrop_set` and `Region.horizon_y` (the region's one horizon, px below the region node). A room's contents scene has one `Backdrop` node (first child of `Contents`) whose `backdrop_set` is null to show the region's set, or a set that overrides it; the anchor is the node's x and the region's horizon y (`Backdrop.shown_set`). A region's set repeats across on every layer, and a set with an edge on y needs a horizon (`backdrop_set_test`). The editor preview of a contents scene opened alone has no Region, so a room without an override shows no backdrop there (accepted). A set is data: its layers far to near (`BackdropLayer`: stacked sheet, `band_count`, `band_of_season`, `scroll_scale` per axis, `horizon_row`, `fill_above`/`fill_below` NONE/EDGE/REPEAT, `repeat_x`, haze `distance`) and `horizon_screen_y`, the eye level in game px.
- **The model, per axis.** At the reference camera (`BackdropMath.reference_centre`: the camera that shows the anchor's horizon at `horizon_screen_y`) every layer stands where it was authored. Away from it a layer's anchor moves by `(camera - reference) * (1 - scroll)`. So a far layer (`scroll` near 0) keeps its horizon at eye level whatever the camera's height, and a layer at 1 is pinned to the world.
- **Built nodes.** `Backdrop.refresh()` builds `LayerN` (`Parallax2D`, `scroll_scale` from the layer, `scroll_offset` from `BackdropMath.scroll_offset` against the room's global position) holding `Art`, a `MeshInstance2D` `QuadMesh` sized to `BackdropMath.cover_rect` (what the room's camera can show at zoom 1 or more, plus 32 px) and clipped by `clip_to_fills`. The built nodes are unowned and never saved. Without a `Room` above it (the editor, a contents scene opened alone) it uses a fixed preview rect.
- **Clipped to its room** (2026-10-09): the current room's neighbours run and draw (`systems/rooms`), and a parallax layer slides past its room's edge, so `Backdrop` sets `room_rect` (the room's world bounds) and the shader discards every pixel drawn outside it (`MODEL_MATRIX * VERTEX` carries the `Parallax2D` scroll). Without it a neighbour's layers drew over the current view and popped at every crossing (`bugs/a-running-neighbour-rooms-backdrop-draws-over-the-current-room`). No `Room` (editor preview): no clip.
- **Room ring** (2026-10-11, `systems/rooms`, `bugs/the-drawn-frame-correction-flips-between-candidates-and-drops-ivo-out-of-frame`, `bugs/the-ring-tie-wedge-draws-a-hard-diagonal-between-two-backdrops`, `bugs/the-ring-dividing-line-leaves-a-one-pixel-diagonal-hole-above-a-seam`): the shader judges a pixel by its centre, `floor(world_pos) + 0.5` (`BackdropMath.pixel_centre`), so two quads never disagree on it (`gotchas/two-quads-disagree-on-a-pixels-world-position`). A pixel in no room goes to `void_owner`, if within `ring` (`RoomRing.WIDTH`, 160 px, by `rect_distance`, the farther-axis distance, so the ring is the square `RoomRing.reach`). Among the rooms within `ring` of the pixel (2026-10-13, `bugs/a-ring-corner-diagonal-shows-a-rock-wall-in-the-mills-sky-on-a-jump`), `void_owner` takes the highest hold, then the smallest gap, then the lower x, then the lower y:
  - ABOVE (3): the room's columns hold the pixel over its top; gap in y. The sky over a room is that room's.
  - BESIDE (2): its rows hold the pixel; gap in x.
  - BELOW (1): its columns hold the pixel under its floor; gap in y. A deeper room beside a floor goes on down under it.
  - CORNER (0): any other room; `rect_distance`.
  Every dividing line is straight: a room edge continued, or a ring limit. The 2026-10-11 nearest-gap rule left a 45-degree line at every L-corner, which the dither hid until WORLD-28. Each room judges among the rooms it touches. Each layer's cover is `RoomRing.reach(bounds)`; the ring reads each layer's `fill_above` and `fill_below` (EDGE on the surface sets, REPEAT on the cave and interior sets). Replaced the same day: per-room 80 px sky rects, then a nearest-room rule with 45-degree seams.
- **Seams are hard cuts, hidden** (WORLD-28; the user, 2026-10-13: "There is no sense in trees there. Nor in the fade between two backgrounds."; choice "One horizon + hidden cuts (Recommended)": "Each region gets one horizon height, so stacked rooms stop showing the trees twice (12 seams fixed by data alone). Where two different backgrounds meet (9 seams), make a hard cut hidden behind something solid (a gatehouse, a cave mouth, a well rim, a wall) and drop the dither."). WORLD-22's Bayer band is gone from `BackdropMath` and the shader together: a pixel in a room is that room's. Rooms of one region at one horizon draw identical pixels, so their seams vanish; a repeating axis is phased on world 0 (`BackdropMath.layer_anchor`). Where pictures differ, `BackdropSeams.cuts` (pure) walks every 32 px cell of the rooms' rings, finds each cell's owner with `keeps`, and reports each run of cell edges between two pictures with no opaque cell (ground, void `_`, `[back]`, lake `w`) on either side; `backdrop_seams_test` fails on a cut whose key (`BackdropCut.key()`: the room pair, `rooms` or `ring`, the axis, line and span, so a new cut between a listed pair is a new key) is not in `ALLOWED`, and on a stale entry. The dividers are `.room` data: `[back]` walls at the square's well shaft, the mill's top row under the waterfall path, the cave mouths of frost_cliffs (both sides), lake_waterfall_path (to the fishing village), lake_high_wall, lake_chasm, orchard_counterweights (side and floor shaft), orchard_cliff_chimney and the crater's two exits, the woods_deep floor hole and the terraces' hole over the canopy. Allowed, each with a reason class checked against the maps and in stills: the fishing village's well mouth (closed by WORLD-29), and straight `ring` cuts in the void outside both rooms: a ring limit facing solid ground, a seam continued under two floors or past two end walls, one under a floor beside a deeper room (a glide shows the strip for about 0.15 s, about 9 frames), one region border over two wall tops (the same open set at two horizons: a climb shows the sky bands step; the user's "OK until skies match (Recommended)"), rings that meet, and the two trial rooms 256 px apart. Side cave-mouth `[back]` columns go one cell into the ground above and below, so the ground's ragged edge lies over the back wall, never over the cut. The user accepted a straight line high in the sky above region-border gatehouses until the region backdrops share one sky colour ("OK until skies match (Recommended)", 2026-10-13); with one interim set there is none today.
- **The shader** picks each pixel's texel from its layer-local position (`VERTEX + quad_position`) with `texelFetch`: EDGE repeats the strip's first or last row, REPEAT wraps, NONE is transparent. Season from the mask (`gh_season_at`, `gh_band_for` in `seasonal_common.gdshaderinc`, shared with `seasonal_art.gdshader`), haze from `distance` exactly as `systems/seasonal-art`. No `TIME`, no `FRAGCOORD`.
- **Measured.** `backdrop_test` drives a real `Camera2D`: each `Parallax2D` lands where the model says at zoom 1 and at 1.5 (the Memorina draw), and a room at (12160, 6000) keeps the vertical pose of one at the origin. `backdrop_math_test` checks one owner per seam pixel for side-by-side rooms, a four-room corner and a room against the void, the hard cut, and `same_picture`.
- **Art contract** (every biome set): `architecture/vertical-backdrops`, "Art contract". Every EDGE row is one colour per band (`backdrop_set_test`). `background_layer_3`'s bottom row was flattened to its dominant colour for this (2026-10-08); its season sources too, so `tools/stack_seasonal_sheets.gd` keeps it.
- **The village valley** (WORLD-11; "They're perfect. It just doesn't have a lot of contrast with the character. Not talking about color itself, because soon I might redraw Ivo (I will, actually). Is there a way to improve that without changing much of the scenario?", user, 2026-10-10). `village_valley.tres` on the four village street rooms: a sky drawn by `tools/art/cut_backdrop.py` (stepped bands, flat clouds) and three Codex layers, snowy mountains (s 0.12, 0.07), hills with fields (0.3, 0.18) and a near tree line (0.55, 0.32), all `horizon_row` 229 and 2x density. Autumn and winter are recolours of the spring drawing. The contrast answer is **atmospheric depth baked into the art**: each band is mixed toward its season's horizon sky by the layer's depth (`AERIAL`: mountains 0.5, hills 0.42, trees 0.32), so the backdrop is lighter and lower in contrast than the play plane whatever Ivo's colours are; the runtime `distance` haze still dissolves it in the grey. The board compared 0, 45/35/22 and 50/42/32 percent; the strongest was kept. The hills' Codex strip ends in a sea cliff with the lighthouse, which cannot wrap, so the tile is cut left of it.
