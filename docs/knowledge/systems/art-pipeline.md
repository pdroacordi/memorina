# Art pipeline: contracts, generation and processing

Moved verbatim from `CLAUDE.md` ("Art pipeline") on 2026-10-02.

Art the game needs is a **contract** before it is a picture: `tools/art/prompts/<id>.md` gives its target path, frame size and frame count, and the generator prompt for it (appended to the one shared look in `tools/art/prompts/style.md`); `tools/art/prompts/README.md` is the format and the loop. `tools/art/make_placeholders.gd` draws the contract's exact sheet so scenes can use it before the art lands, and `art_prompt_test.gd` fails while any target is missing or mis-sized. Stills are drawn by Codex CLI (the `codex-consult` skill's image mode, on magenta, in the scratchpad); animations from a reference by `pixellab.ps1 generate -Prompt <id>`. `tools/art/process_image.gd` then keys the background out (an explicit `--key` everywhere, enclosed holes included), crops the frames to their shared bounds, snaps to `tools/art/palette.json` (extracted from the world art by `extract_palette.gd`) at full size, and scales each by ONE factor into its frame, bottom-centre, keeping each block's most common colour on a large shrink - proportions are never stretched, so a picture off the contract's aspect is re-prompted, not distorted. Pure steps live in `ImageOps` (tested). **One pixel density: everything is drawn at 2x** ("One 2x pixel density + Orbita-style rules (Recommended)", user, 2026-10-10). One art pixel is 2x2 game pixels, as in Ivo's sprites: a 32 px tile is drawn at 16 art px, a 96 px sprite at 48. A 2x character on 1x ground read as wrong (the FAG-orbita comparison); `tools/art/prompts/style.md` carries the prompt rules that came with it (16-bit, hard pixels, limited palette, medium brightness, no baked light, generate small). A tile recipe's `"art_scale": 2` makes `assemble_tile_sheet.gd` compose at 16 px (`WangSheet.use_scale`, `TilePainter.use_scale`) and double the sheet, nearest, to the 128x960 contract. Props and backdrops drawn at 1x before this are to be redrawn at 2x.

**Codex pictures are cut by Python tools** (2026-10-10; run with `python -I`, numpy, PIL, scipy): `tools/art/cut_facades.py` (building fronts and the door, each scaled by its doorway), `cut_backdrop.py` (a landscape strip into a seamless 3-band layer, season recolours and baked atmospheric depth; the sky), `cut_wall.py` (a texture into a seamless one-band wall) and `cut_inhabitant.py` (a PixelLab loop onto the 2x grid in its still's colours; `--keep` picks the returned frames, `--frame`/`--at` pad each onto a larger square, as Ivo's 128 px sheets do, `--floor` puts each frame's feet on a row and `--centre` holds its body on the flip axis; Ivo's attacks are built by `build_ivo_attacks.py` from key-pose stills, smears and the run's legs, `architecture/ivo-at-2x`). Each keeps the 2x density, reduces by each block's most common colour, and never blends pixels: a wrap is stitched along the cut of least difference. The raw pictures stay in `tools/art/sources/` (`.gdignore`d, so Godot never imports them); every contract names its tool's command line.

**Biome tile sheets are composed from recipes** (WORLD-07, 2026-10-10): a look's recipe, `tools/art/tiles/<biome>_<look>.json`, names a body (`TilePainter`: periodic noise, Voronoi stones, planks, plaster and timber; every body repeats with a 32 px period, and fill variants change nothing inside a 4 px margin, so they tile with the plain body and each other), the fill variants, one lip per season band (`WangSheet.blade_strip`, `litter_strip`, `snow_strip`, `cobble_strip`, layered) and the edge style. `tools/art/assemble_tile_sheet.gd --recipe=<json> --out=<png>` composes the 16 dual-grid tiles with `WangSheet.compose` (rounded outer corners, a dark edge, a rim, the lip laid along every exposed top and following the corner's curve). Palettes are sampled from the approved style picture. PixelLab's v2 sidescroller tileset (`pixellab.ps1 tileset`) was measured on the village earth twice (2026-10-10, with and without the concept as a reference): its top is a solid moss block filling the exposed quadrant, nothing like the concept, so it is kept only as a body source (`"kind": "pixellab"` in a recipe). The bodies come from PixelLab tiles-pro instead (`pixellab.ps1 tiles`: square top-down tiles in segmentation mode, a crop of the approved picture shrunk by averaging to one tile as the style tile; 16 px at the 2x density): four seamless variations per material, kept in `tools/art/tiles/sources/` and named by a recipe's `"path"` ("Try to use pixel lab instead of codex", user, 2026-10-10). The village earth and cobbles were then redrawn by Codex ("Try again using codex with the new guides", user, 2026-10-10; "Good, redo cobbles via Codex too (Recommended)", user, 2026-10-10): one sheet of 16 art px tiles on magenta, sliced on the gutters, each reduced by box averaging and quantized; the raw is kept beside the sources (`*_codex_raw.png`). Each variant body is composed into the fill and the four straight edges, so a street one row deep varies too; the lip is shared, so its tufts still repeat every 32 px. The painted bodies (`TilePainter`) remain for looks the generators miss. Never generate into `assets/`, never upload pack art as a reference (the GandalfHardcore and ZeggyGames licences forbid AI training), and credit everything in `CREDITS.md`.

**Props tools** (WORLD-11, 2026-10-13): `cut_props.py` trims every object to its opaque bounds (a dropped keyed fringe left a transparent bottom row, so props floated 2 px; `bugs/cut-props-left-a-transparent-bottom-row-so-props-floated`); `cut_facades.py --palette=<pngs>` snaps a later front to the colours the built fronts use; `clean_prop.py` turns a PixelLab still drawn at art size (pixflux with our cut as the init image: the way to redraw a prop that turned to mush when shrunk) into a 2x prop; `trim_strip.py` crops a strip's frames to their shared bounds; `widen_strip.py` repeats one plain art column in every frame (a string between flags); `season_bands.py` stacks a nature prop's spring art with autumn and winter recolours of its foliage; `draw_ambient_frames.py` draws lit panes from a front's dark panes, lantern flames, a sign's swing and chimney smoke; `draw_pictograms.py` draws picture notes pixel by pixel; `draw_post.py` draws a squared wooden post (the bridge piles, the bunting pole, the fishing village's pier posts) and, with `--foot`, a post's foot in a lake (WORLD-29); `bake_rotation.gd` (pure `RotationBake`) bakes a turn into frames, reading the still through one symmetry sector so the loop closes, and prints the wrap ratio. Contracts take a `bands:` key (sheet = `size.x * frames` by `size.y * bands`).

**Region tiles and backdrops** (WORLD-07, 2026-10-13; boards approved with "Approve, fix the flat ground (Recommended)", user, 2026-10-13; the boards are in `tools/art/references/regions/`). Each region's looks are a `BiomeTileset` (`threshold`, `frost_edge`, `lighthouse`, `spring_woods`, `frozen_lake`, `wind_orchards`, `summer_caldera`, `summer_caldera_ash`), each look a recipe `tools/art/tiles/<biome>_<look>.json` over 16 art px bodies in `tools/art/tiles/sources/`, composed by `assemble_tile_sheet.gd` as the village is. Codex draws one tile sheet per region (our village bodies, enlarged, the only reference), but it draws about 32 art px per tile whatever the prompt says; shrunk to 16 a mottled earth turns to speckle. So:
- a body whose shapes survive the shrink (rock, woods earth and rock, lake earth) is cut from the sheet by `cut_tile_bodies.py`: the tile rolled by half wraps at its edges, its middle seam is cut out along the paths of least difference to the unrolled tile, and each variant keeps the base's outer 2 art px ring and takes the variant tile's middle along a minimum cut (scipy max-flow), so every variant tiles with the base and the others. `--recolour` maps finished bodies onto another value ramp: "One rock, two palettes (Recommended)" (user, 2026-10-13) is the Frost Edge rock recoloured blue for the lake, and the lake's planks are the village planks recoloured grey.
- every other body is painted at 16 art px by `paint_tile_bodies.py` from a spec in `tools/art/tiles/bodies/` (soil, slabs, ashlar, drystone, basalt, scree), in colours sampled from the region's Codex sheet; everything wraps on the 16 px period. A soil's base is a calm mottle and its stones and roots live in the variants: the fill draws a variant in 4 cells of 10 (`GroundAutotile.fill_variant`), so a stone in the base repeats as wallpaper every 32 px. Every detail is placed by its variant's seed, and the painter refuses two bodies closer than 6 art px; `biome_tileset_test` fails any look whose fills differ by fewer than 3 (`bugs/painted-tile-variants-repeat-because-fixed-position-details-ignore-their-seed`).
- A look's `Enums.Ground` is its legend character's, never the biome's: the Caldera's ash scree ("Caldera ash as scree", user, 2026-10-13) is the STONE sheet of its own biome, `summer_caldera_ash`, so its cells are `S` and roots never take it; the lighthouse's white masonry ("Lighthouse masonry tiles") is the STONE sheet of `lighthouse`. A fifth look (ASH or MASONRY beside basalt or rock in one room) would need a `Look` value, a legend character and a `FORMAT_VERSION` bump (`architecture/biome-tilesets`).
- Backdrops: one `BackdropSet` per region (`resources/world/backdrops/<region>.tres`), the village valley's sky and its horizon (row 229, eye level 191, scrolls 0.05 / 0.12 / 0.3 / 0.55), three Codex layers cut by `cut_backdrop.py`. Every layer is drawn in spring and its winter is a recolour ("Draw spring, recolour to winter (Recommended)", user, 2026-10-13): `--kind=sea` turns open water to ice, `--kind=pines` keeps firs green, `--kind=broadleaf` turns winter crowns to snow and bare branches, `--snow=N` caps every top edge with N art px of snow, `--aerial` sets the layer's atmospheric depth. A layer with nothing above it (`fill_above` NONE) closes its shapes inside the strip: its silhouette top is never art row 0 (`bugs/the-spring-woods-near-trees-are-cut-flat-at-the-strip-top`, `backdrop_set_test`). Rooms inside rock get a wall set cut by `cut_wall.py`: `sea_cliff_rock`, `lake_cliff_rock`, `orchard_limestone` (beside `earth_caves`, `ice_caves`, `basalt_caves`).
- Wired 2026-10-13 (WORLD-07, WORLD-30): every room names its region's biome and every region shows its set (`systems/rooms`, Tilesets per region; `systems/backdrops`, Today's sets). "The pack tiles leave as each region is done." (user, 2026-10-13): no scene draws pack ground tiles or pack backgrounds now; the pack files stay on disk, unused (`CREDITS.md`), and `legacy.tres` stays for the parser test fixtures.

## Creatures (CORE-13, CORE-04; 2026-10-29)

- **A Codex sheet needs a grid-aware cut.** Codex draws at about 12-14 source px per drawn pixel; resampling straight to the target size turns a small creature to mush. `tools/art/cut_creatures.py` measures each creature's own pixel grid, reads each drawn pixel once, then reduces to `--size`. Where that is still mush, PixelLab pixflux redraws it at exact size with the cut as the init image (the `clean_prop.py` route).
- **animate-with-text-v3 paints effects into frames:** white notes, flashes, grey backdrops, a turn to face east or the back. `cut_inhabitant.py --drop=D` clears pixels far from the still's colours and `--ceiling=ROW` clears above a row; reject a clip that turned and retry.
- **One strip per clip:** `ArtPrompt` allows 1 or 3 bands, so a species is one `<name>_<clip>.png` strip per clip, frames side by side, feet on the bottom row; `tools/art/pad_reference.py` places a 2x still on the frame canvas on even pixels.

## Guardians (GRD-07; 2026-10-29)

- **A guardian sheet is 1 px per art px**, shown at `Sprite2D` scale 2 (Bloom 128x96, Frost 192x128 frames). `cut_inhabitant.py --scale=1` reads and writes at that density; `--write=1` reads 2x PixelLab frames and writes 1x. The slender Bloom stag (22x60 art px) animated badly at 1x; from a 2x reference (256x192, PixelLab's 256 px limit) it held its profile. Frost at 2x would be 384 px, so it animates at 1x.
- **Attacks come back with effects in the body's own colours** (ice bursts in the crystals' blues, pale whip arcs near the stag's skin), which `--drop` cannot clear, and with turns to face the viewer; asking for "no effects" or "facing left the whole time" did not stop it. What worked: keep the clean frames and compose the clip with `--keep` (`0-8,8-8,7-5,1-1`), so the hitbox window still lands on the blow.
- PixelLab's Tier 1 allows 8 concurrent background jobs; a ninth returns 429.
- A still with a near-white pixel puts PixelLab's white trails within `--drop` range of its own colours. Cut against a palette strip of the still's colours without the white (`tools/art/sources/guardians/bloom_palette.png`, passed as the still), and `--drop=45` clears them.
- **Round 2 (2026-10-29): what moved a clip, and what did not.** A 16-frame generation gives room to compose a 14-frame attack whose hit window lands on the blow (`--keep` picks wind-up, impact and recovery). Wording the pose in body terms worked: "the fist just above the floor", "knees pulled up high to its chest", "lands upright on its two hind legs". Three things did not move in two retries each: the Frost walk turns from the still's three-quarter view to profile mid-cycle (the loop is composed from its three-quarter frames only); the Frost hurt and a second slam painted ice bursts and blocks in the crystals' blues; the Bloom stamp lands on all fours like a deer.
- `tools/art/despeckle.py <strip> <frame width> [--min=6]` clears the loose specks a cut leaves (a dashed whip needs `--min=4`). `tools/art/lean_frames.py` makes a recoil from one still by leaning it back above a pivot row, where every generated recoil carried an effect.
- The lucidity leap has its own strip (`<name>_leap.png`, 8 frames: crouch, launch, flight, land, stand), feet on the floor row like every other clip, since the arc is the guardian's own motion. The flight lasts about 1.67 s, so the clip holds or loops its flight frames until the landing.
